Where the numbers come from

Nothing on this page is decorative. Each beat is priced out from the same link budget the mission is planned with, at the slant range the payload actually occupies at that moment of flight.

d_slant = √(r₁² + r₂² − 2r₁r₂cosθ) — earth curvature, not flat-earth
RSSI = 22 + 2 − FSPL − 3pol − 1misc + 10yagi − 1.2feed
BER = ½·exp(−γ/2) non-coherent FSK, γ = Eb/N₀
PER = 1 − (1 − BER)8·213 — CRC-16 rejects any bit error

The beat itself is the McSharry–Clifford sum-of-Gaussians ECG model — the ecgsyn generator published on PhysioNet — using its P-Q-R-S-T wave positions and widths, with amplitudes set to standard limb-lead proportions and then driven by link margin instead of by heart rate.

The rhythm axis is literal. Beat-to-beat spacing is the true packet period: 44.4 ms of airtime for 213 bytes at 38.4 kb/s, plus the 60 ms GAP_MS the transmitter waits so the receiver can keep up. That is 104.4 ms per transaction — a resting rate of 575 beats per minute. Placement inside a complex is schematic, the way a real ECG is drawn at a conventional 25 mm/s.
The cliff
Packet loss vs slant range

Digital links do not fade, they fall over. Between 200 and 300 km the link goes from losing six packets in a thousand to losing all of them — on a signal that only dropped 3.5 dB.

Wide QRS, always. A healthy heart spends about 11% of its cycle in the QRS. HAB-1 spends 43% — 44.4 ms of payload in a 104.4 ms beat. On a real ECG that is a bundle branch block. Here it just means the duty cycle is high, which is exactly what you want from a link you are paying airtime for.
