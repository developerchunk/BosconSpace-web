SIM800L on two LiPos and a 2 A buck

Two 3.7 V cells in series make about 7.4 V. A 2 A buck (step-down, such as an LM2596 board) turns that into a steady 4.0 V for the SIM800L. The 1000 µF capacitor goes at the SIM800L end of the buck's output wires. This is the setup the common SIM800L tutorial recommends: "a 2A-rated DC-DC buck converter, such as the LM2596, with the output voltage set to 4.0V" (lastminuteengineers). The SIM800L accepts 3.4 to 4.4 V (SIMCom).

1. Check how your two cells are joined

Series: + of cell 1 joined to − of cell 2. The meter across the pack reads about 7.4 to 8.4 V. this is what the buck needs

Parallel: + to + and − to −. The meter reads about 3.7 to 4.2 V. A buck cannot make 4.0 V from that. not for the buck Rejoin them in series.

Before joining two cells, charge both full (each about 4.2 V on the meter, within 0.05 V of each other). Two cells at different charge should not be joined. Each cell should have its own protection board (the small PCB under the wrap).

2. The full wiring

3. Set the buck to 4.0 V before the SIM800L is connected

  1. Connect only the pack to the buck: pack + to IN+, pack − to IN−. Nothing on OUT yet.
  2. Meter on DC volts across OUT+ and OUT−.
  3. Turn the small brass screw on the blue trimmer slowly, a few turns at a time, until the meter reads 4.0 V (anything from 3.9 to 4.1 V is fine).
  4. Never above 4.4 V on the SIM800L: that is its limit (SIMCom). Many of these boards come set to a high voltage, so always measure first.
  5. Disconnect the pack again before the next step.

4. Connect, in this order

  1. Remove the old single-LiPo wires from the SIM800L's VCC and GND. The pack and buck replace them.
  2. Buck OUT+ to SIM800L VCC, buck OUT− to SIM800L GND, with short wires (a few cm), soldered.
  3. Capacitor at the SIM800L end: + leg to VCC, stripe (−) leg to GND, as close to the module as you can. If it is already fitted there, keep it.
  4. Shared ground: SIM800L GND (or buck OUT−) also to a NodeMCU GND pin, as now.
  5. Signal wires unchanged: SIM800L TXD to D1, RXD to D2, RST to D6.
  6. Meter on continuity across SIM800L VCC and GND: a short beep that stops is fine (capacitor charging); a beep that does not stop is a short.
  7. Power on: pack to buck first, then the NodeMCU (power bank). Press the NodeMCU's RST button once so it starts cleanly.

The buck's IN− and OUT− are the same ground on these boards, so the pack −, the buck and the NodeMCU all share one ground. That is what the TXD/RXD wires need.
