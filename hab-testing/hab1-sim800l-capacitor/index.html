Fitting the capacitor on the SIM800L

One capacitor, two solder joints: its + leg on VCC and its − leg on GND, right at the module. Every other wire stays exactly where it is. Why: the modem restarts when it first transmits (2 A bursts); SIMCom's reference design puts a capacitor across VCC and GND at the module to carry those bursts.

1. Which leg is + on your capacitor

Electrolytic (a can, like "1000µF 16V"): the long leg is +, the side with the stripe of − signs is −. use this

Tantalum (small yellow block, "107" = 100 µF): the stripe or bar marks +, the opposite of an electrolytic. also fine

Small disc marked "104" or "105": only 0.1 or 1 µF, far too small on its own. If yours looks like this, stop and tell me what is printed on it. not this

2. Where it goes

Pin names as on the common SIM800L module: check the labels printed on your board and match VCC and GND by name, not by position. The capacitor's legs pass over RST, RXD and TXD: cover them with heat-shrink or tape so they cannot touch those pins.

3. Steps

  1. Power everything off. Unplug LiPo B from the SIM800L, and the power bank or USB from the NodeMCU.
  2. Find + on the capacitor (section 1).
  3. Solder the + leg to the VCC pin, next to the LiPo B red wire already there.
  4. Solder the − leg to the GND pin, next to the LiPo B black wire and the Y-lead already there.
  5. Keep the capacitor right at the module, legs as short as they can be. A capacitor far away on long wires does little.
  6. Insulate both legs with heat-shrink or tape.
  7. Check for a short: meter on continuity across VCC and GND. A short beep that stops is the capacitor charging, fine. A beep that does not stop is a short: find it before powering on.
  8. Power on: LiPo B to the SIM800L first, then the NodeMCU (USB to the Mac is best for this test). Board at the window.

An electrolytic fitted the wrong way round can swell or burst. Check + and − twice before you power it.
